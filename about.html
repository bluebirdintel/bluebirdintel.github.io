About

> how the bird learned to fly_

After years on a casino floor, I noticed my skillset was being held back by handling voucher thefts, stopping drunken fights, and paying large jackpots. I wanted to make a change and chase a dream I'd had since I was a teenager.

See, I was good at talking to people and handling customer service issues, but I was great at manually tracing stolen vouchers across the floor to the thief, and at building profiles for the executives when guests' problematic behavior warranted longer exclusions. After a decade on the floor, I thought that dream was more achievable.

I wanted to learn how to hack, and I thought the best way to do that and make good money was by falling for the great cybersecurity bootcamp marketing scheme. I dropped $8k on a bootcamp that I just as quickly dropped out of after seeing I wasn't guaranteed the selling point of a "guaranteed job" after completing it. I switched to self-study with CompTIA and earned my Security+ certification in 2024. I'm currently pursuing CySA+ the same way.

Along the job-search journey, I fell in love with watching scambaiting. I wanted to do something similar, even if I didn't get on camera myself (which ended up happening anyway). Then I realized that all those years at the casino, I was doing OSINT for the behavior profiles, so maybe I could do the same for scams.

So the bird began to fly.

I started with a recruiter who reached out about casino work with some pretty odd behavior, and then a group using social media to push a strangely registered service they owned, tied to a crypto they also owned, with some extremely shady behavior around it. That turned into a year of learning the basics of blockchain forensics, learning how to read business registries, and cross-referencing everything to build an investigation I later turned over to the authorities.

I've done misinformation research to disprove false information spreading on social media, deep-dived bot accounts to learn and teach what to look for and how to trace their images back to the source using open-source tools, and built some frameworks along the way.

I'm currently building more tools to solidify my knowledge and help others, and I'm also open to work. If you'd like to talk, reach me at bluebirdintel@proton.me.
